# Designing a Zero-to-One Landing Experience for Lesson Planner PH

> **Outcome:** Built the foundational landing page from a blank slate to help Filipino teachers understand the tool in seconds and give Symph a credible presence for school outreach.

---

## 1. The Problem

Filipino teachers spend countless unpaid hours every week manually formatting lesson plans (Daily Lesson Logs). That administrative burden takes time away from actual classroom teaching.

Symph was developing **Lesson Planner PH** to automate this process, but the product had **no website, no brand assets, and zero online presence**. 

To launch successfully, the landing page had to solve two challenges simultaneously:
1. **Explain the product instantly to teachers:** Educators needed to see immediately that the tool would save them real time, without being overwhelmed by technical jargon.
2. **Build credibility for institutions:** School administrators and principals needed to trust that the outputs complied with curriculum standards before recommending it to their staff.

---

## 2. The Before: Starting From Scratch

Instead of jumping straight into high-fidelity mockups or colors, I started by wireframing the narrative structure to test how the story flowed from top to bottom.

## 3. The Core Decisions (And Why)

### Decision 1: Wireframe the narrative and align stakeholders first
Before opening any visual design tools, I mapped out a 4-step narrative sequence on wireframes:
1. **The Hook:** Directly acknowledging teacher paperwork fatigue.
2. **The How-It-Works:** Showing a simple 3-step generation process.
3. **The Proof:** Highlighting curriculum-compliant lesson outputs.
4. **The Next Step:** Offering a free trial for teachers alongside a contact channel for schools.

> **Why:** Jumping straight to visual design often leads to debating button colors instead of fixing information flow. Presenting structural wireframes to the Product Manager and Engineering team ensured we were 100% aligned on scope and copy hierarchy before styling.

### Decision 2: Focus on "time saved" rather than technical buzzwords
Rather than pitching complex AI algorithms, the copy and visual hierarchy focused entirely on the outcome: **getting your Sunday nights back**.

> **Why:** Teachers don't adopt software because it uses advanced tech; they adopt it because it solves an immediate pain point. Showing an actual preview of a formatted lesson plan proved the value faster than any paragraph of text could.

## 5. What Was Measured (And What Would Prove Me Wrong)

**What we tracked:**
- Teacher free trial sign-ups from the landing page
- Inbound inquiries from schools and department heads
- Scroll depth to see if visitors reached the feature breakdown

> **What would have proved me wrong:**  
> If visitors landed on the page but didn't click the primary action, it would have shown that leading with the "time-saving" hook wasn't clear enough, or that teachers were hesitant without seeing more sample lesson plans upfront.
