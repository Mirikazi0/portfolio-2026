### Section 4: "The problem" (The Data Contrast)
- **Handwritten Header:** `The problem` ✍️
- **Big Typographic Metric Hook:**
  ```
  49% engaged. (Vibrant Blue)
   6% shopping. (Dark Neutral)
  ```
- **The Core Diagnosis:**
  > *"The Walmart Africa homepage had a healthy engagement rate. Users were showing up, scrolling, tapping around. The problem was where the shopping actually happened — and it wasn't on the homepage."*
- **Funnel Drop-off Metrics:**
  - **49%** `HOMEPAGE ENGAGEMENT RATE` — *users showing up*
  - **6–7%** `HOMEPAGE → CART ADDITIONS` — *users actually shopping*
  - **40%** `SEARCH → CART ADDITIONS` — *where action happens*
  - **13%** `PDP → CART ADDITIONS` — *product pages*
- **The Punchline:**
  > *"Users were showing up to the homepage and then going somewhere else to actually shop. The homepage was doing the first job of a homepage, pulling people in, and failing the second one."*

### Section 5: "The evidence" (Component Performance Audit)
- **Handwritten Header:** `The evidence` ✍️
- **Core Headline:**
  > *"High exposure did not equal high product action. Some modules earned their placement. Most were coasting on scroll depth."*
- **Module Performance Breakdown:**
  - **🟢 EARNS ITS SPOT (High Conversion & Action):**
    1. **Top Sellers carousel:** Highest volume on homepage, 971 clicks (1st Fold).
    2. **Around the World carousel:** Highest rate on homepage, 1.15% CTR (2nd Fold).
    3. **Sweets & Gum:** Small category, outsized performance, High CTR + ATC (3rd Fold).
    4. **Fresh Butchery:** High volume, downstream conversion, Strong ATC value (2nd Fold).
  - **🔴 LOW CONTRIBUTION (Wasted Visual Real Estate):**
    1. **Liquor Adjustable Banner:** Prominent placement, minimal action, 0.12% CTR (Last Fold).
    2. **Multiple generic banners:** Impressions only – no product action, 0.00% ATC (Last Fold).

### Section 6: "Research & Competitive synthesis"
- **Handwritten Header:** `Research & Competitive synthesis` ✍️
- **Subtitle:** `Five design implications from the research.`
- **The 5 Strategic Principles:**
  1. **Shoppers start with savings.**  
     *Deals, specials, and value cues aren't secondary they're the entry point. Price comparison happens on the homepage, sometimes while a user is standing inside a competitor's store.*
  2. **Grocery shopping is repeat-heavy.**  
     *Buy Again, favourites, and reorder flows aren't features they're infrastructure. Users want to rebuild routine baskets, not rediscover products every session.*
  3. **Relevance beats volume.**  
     *Curated, timely collections outperform generic category rows in both competitor apps and our research. Context beats taxonomy.*
  4. **Speed is part of the value proposition.**  
     *Quick-commerce users want to get in and out. Long, banner-heavy homepages feel slower. Scroll depth is not engagement — it's friction dressed up as time-on-page.*
  5. **Quality is an unclaimed position.**  
     *Checkers owns convenience. Pick n Pay owns value. Nobody in the SA market owns product quality. That's a gap Walmart Africa could claim and none of the design decisions below make sense without it.*

## PART III: THE COMPLETE DESIGN ENGINEERING PLAYBOOK

### 1. Typography & Visual Rules
- **Display Typography:** Serif headings (e.g. *Newsreader*, *Playfair*, or *Fraunces*) paired with clean sans-serif UI typography (*Figtree* or *Inter*).
- **Handwritten Accents:** Casual handwritten annotations (e.g. *Caveat* or custom SVG doodles) for section titles, underlines, and sticky notes.
- **Blueprint Grid Background:** Faint geometric/isometric grid lines to give designs an architectural/engineering precision.

### 2. Narrative Framing Rules
- **Avoid:** "I conducted 5 user interviews and made personas..."
- **Use:** "At a company like Walmart, redesigns don't get funded on ambition — they get funded on evidence."
- **Audit Components with Metrics:** Never show UI without labeling whether each component *"Earns its spot"* or is *"Low contribution"*.
- **Acknowledge Code & Design Systems:** Mentioning tokens, design system repositories (like *Vibey* in VS Code), and data tools (Power BI, SQL) signals instant Design Engineer seniority.
