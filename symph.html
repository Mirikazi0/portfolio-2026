What this project was and wasn't

Architecture before aesthetics.

The brief I received had three words of direction: highlight the value. No wireframes, existing website, or reference UI existed. I had to build a landing page for a product that helped Filipino teachers generate lesson plans in minutes, making its value clear to the team and users.

In practice, I structured the architecture before defining aesthetics. I determined the story sequence for two audiences, individual teachers and school administrators, ensuring clear communication for both.

I started with structure: wireframed the narrative, presented it to the PM and engineers, iterated on copy through feedback, and then moved into high-fidelity design. Figma came in after locking the story.

The problem

A product that worked. A page that didn't exist.

A product that worked.
A page that didn't exist.

Lesson Planner PH helped teachers generate structured lesson plans faster. The product worked, but without a landing page, administrators, buyers, and international visitors could not evaluate what it was, why it mattered, or whether to trust it.

The landing page needed to give individual teachers a clear reason to try it while providing institutions a rational reason to recommend it.

Audience tension

Two audiences. One page.

Different stakeholders needed completely different signals to commit:

FILIPINO TEACHERS
"Will this save me time?"

Generic pages lose them. Tech jargon and missing emotional hooks signal a tool that wasn't built for their actual reality.

"Can we trust this institutionally?"

Informal tone and missing compliance signals cause school leaders to bounce before a B2B conversation starts.

The structural decision: build a 4-stage narrative covering problem acknowledgment, workflow, evidence, and dual CTA. Teacher trust first, institutional credibility second.

Research synthesis

Three structural decisions.

1.
Lead with the problem, not the product.

"Your Sunday nights back" lands harder than "AI-powered lesson planning." Teachers buy time savings and relief rather than software specs.

2.
Structure before aesthetics.

Every wireframe review meeting focused on information flow rather than visual style. The copy and sequence were locked before selecting colors.

3.
Framing outcomes serves both audiences.

"Compliant with DepEd standards" serves the institution. "Done in minutes" serves the teacher. The same design does both without compromising either.

One landing page.
Endless doors opened.

Before launch, the product had no face for non-teaching users. Shipping the landing page created a shared narrative for school administrators, B2B partners, and international visitors.
